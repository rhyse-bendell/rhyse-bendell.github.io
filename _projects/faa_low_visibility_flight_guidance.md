## Project Overview

{% include project_overview_figure.html %}

This FAA-funded aviation human-factors study examined how different forms of flight-deck guidance affect pilot performance during low-visibility instrument approaches and landings. The research was coordinated by Daniela Kratchounova, Ph.D., at the FAA's Civil Aerospace Medical Institute (CAMI), with analytical and reporting contributions from university researchers. The work used a Boeing 737-800 full-flight simulator and professional flight crews to evaluate flight performance across combinations of guidance technology, runway visual range, decision height, and runway lighting.

The broader research program addressed the instrument segment, visual segment, and landing and rollout phases of instrument landing system (ILS) approaches. Its practical motivation was to build an empirical understanding of the performance implications of modern flight-deck displays and guidance systems, including the conditions under which they support accurate, safe approaches and landings.

## Research Design

The studies compared conventional instrument information with increasingly integrated guidance configurations, including:

- Raw-data guidance on head-down flight displays;
- Flight director guidance on head-down displays;
- Flight director information presented on both head-down displays and head-up displays (HUDs); and
- Additional HUD guidance capabilities, including AIII-mode guidance.

The experimental design varied runway visual range and associated decision heights, as well as runway lighting configurations. It also included normal operations and selected abnormal situations, such as loss of flight-director guidance and deviations in navigation signals.

These manipulations enabled comparisons of guidance-system performance across operational conditions rather than treating the presence of a HUD as a simple yes-or-no variable.

## Performance Measures and Analyses

The analysis drew on simulator flight-path data to characterize pilot performance across the approach-to-landing sequence. Measures included localizer and glideslope deviation, flight technical error, threshold crossing behavior, touchdown-point dispersion, distance from the runway threshold, centerline deviation, rollout performance, and go-around decisions.

The analytical work combined data preparation, descriptive and inferential statistics, comparisons across experimental conditions, and visualization of flight trajectories and performance outcomes. Flight-path plots provided an additional perspective on how aircraft trajectories varied across guidance conditions and normal versus abnormal operations.

My role focused on statistical analysis and visualization of flight-performance data, including results from the instrument and visual approach segments and touchdown measures.

These analyses informed an evidence-based evaluation of the operational implications of flight-deck guidance technologies. The findings were not limited to the presence or absence of a HUD: performance depended on the phase of flight, the measure being assessed, and the experimental configuration.
