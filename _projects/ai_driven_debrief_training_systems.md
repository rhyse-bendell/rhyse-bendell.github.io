## Project Overview

{% include project_overview_figure.html %}

**AI-Driven Debrief for Training Systems** is a technology assessment and demonstration-facilitation effort supporting the modernization of naval aviation training. Conducted through [Central Florida Tech Grove](https://centralfloridatechgrove.org/) and the University of Central Florida's [Institute for Simulation and Training](https://www.ist.ucf.edu/), the work supports the needs of the Navy's [Naval Aviation Training Systems and Ranges Program Office (PMA-205)](https://www.navair.navy.mil/organization/PMA-205) in coordination with the [Naval Air Warfare Center Training Systems Division (NAWCTSD)](https://www.navair.navy.mil/nawctsd).

Training debriefs and after-action reviews (AARs) are essential opportunities for instructors and trainees to reconstruct what happened, identify performance gaps, and turn an experience into improved future decisions. Yet interpreting a complex training event can require substantial instructor time and effort, particularly when relevant observations are distributed across simulation logs, trainee actions, mission events, and other performance records. The central question for this project is how mature AI-enabled technologies could help transform such information into accurate, useful, and instructionally meaningful feedback.

The emphasis is **identifying, characterizing, and demonstrating existing capabilities**, not developing or integrating a new training platform under this project order. Technology transition and acquisition decisions remain with the government.

## Technology Assessment and Instructional Requirements

A major activity is translating operational training and debriefing needs into observable evaluation criteria. Working with program representatives, instructors, and other subject-matter experts, the team is establishing what information instructors need to assess a mission and what useful assistance an AI-enabled debrief could realistically provide.

The evaluation framework considers several related questions:

- **Event interpretation:** Can a technology organize training-event information into an accurate account of important actions, conditions, and deviations?
- **Narrative quality and evidence:** Are AI-generated explanations understandable, sufficiently specific, and traceable to underlying observations?
- **Instructional usefulness:** Does the output help instructors identify teachable moments, support constructive feedback, and encourage trainee reflection?
- **Usability and oversight:** Can instructors inspect, qualify, and supplement automated analyses without surrendering their professional judgment?
- **Technical feasibility:** What data inputs, interfaces, processing requirements, and deployment constraints would affect a candidate system's suitability?

These dimensions help distinguish a compelling demonstration from a capability likely to be useful in an actual training and debriefing workflow.

## Market Discovery and Capability Demonstrations

The project includes structured outreach to commercial, academic, and nontraditional technology providers to identify potentially relevant AI-assisted debrief products and components. Candidate capabilities are characterized against common requirements, with attention to what vendors can substantiate rather than what they merely claim.

A planned multi-vendor demonstration event will provide a common setting for observing capabilities and gathering feedback from instructors, technical specialists, and other stakeholders. This approach is intended to make capability comparisons more transparent while surfacing gaps, constraints, and opportunities for future government-directed evaluation.

The work will culminate in a consolidated market-research and demonstration findings package, along with a **non-binding technical transition assessment**. These are advisory products; the project does not perform source selection, award decisions, or direct software engineering for the fielded systems.

## My Contributions

My involvement focuses on the human-factors and research-evaluation aspects of the effort: clarifying debrief information needs, helping translate those needs into defensible measures, engaging subject-matter experts and potential technology providers, and supporting the design of structured capability demonstrations and evidence-based assessments.

This work builds on my broader research in human–AI interaction, cognitive systems, performance measurement, and simulation-based experimentation. It is an opportunity to apply those methods to a practical instructional challenge: ensuring that AI assistance improves the quality and efficiency of debriefing without sacrificing the expertise and oversight of instructors.
