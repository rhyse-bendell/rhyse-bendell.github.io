## Project Overview

{% include project_overview_figure.html %}

**Building a Problem Space for Science Translation: Team Science and the Translation of Research Findings for Application in the Real World** is an [Army Research Office](https://www.arl.army.mil/)-supported research project examining why promising scientific discoveries often fail to become effective practices, policies, or operational capabilities. The project is led by Dr. Stephen M. Fiore at the [University of Central Florida's Cognitive Sciences Laboratory](https://csl.ist.ucf.edu/) with Dr. Scott Rosas of [Concept Systems, Inc.](https://conceptsystems.com/team/) and collaborators across the research-translation community. The [CSL research listing](https://csl.ist.ucf.edu/Research) identifies the project period as July 2026 through June 2028.

Research translation is often described as a movement from discovery through development, demonstration, dissemination, and practical use. In reality, this process traverses many interdependent social systems: scientists and engineers, funding organizations, industry partners, practitioners, decision-makers, and operational communities. Breakdowns can occur when these groups bring different priorities, disciplinary languages, time horizons, success criteria, or understandings of what a research finding means in practice.

Rather than treating the persistent research-to-practice gap solely as a technical or funding problem, this project conceptualizes translation as a **complex, collaborative, and temporally unfolding problem of team science**.

## Research Questions and Objectives

The research seeks to develop a generalizable, multi-level framework that explains how scientific knowledge is translated across organizational and professional boundaries. Its central objectives are to:

1. **Characterize contextual influences:** Identify individual, team, and organizational factors that enable or inhibit research translation, including disciplinary assumptions, stakeholder relationships, and institutional constraints.
2. **Understand temporal dependencies:** Examine how decisions, handoffs, and coordination practices unfold across translation stages and how early problems can accumulate or propagate.
3. **Model translation as a social-cognitive system:** Integrate research from translational science, implementation science, and the science of team science to identify mechanisms and possible leverage points for better knowledge transfer.

The intended result is not a generic checklist. It is a structured representation of the translation problem space that can account for interactions among actors, contexts, knowledge, and processes.

## Group Concept Mapping Approach

A central methodological component is **Group Concept Mapping (GCM)**, a participatory mixed-method approach for eliciting and structuring the perspectives of stakeholders who experience different parts of the problem. The project brings together experts from research, engineering, industry, policy, and operational communities.

Activities include:

- **Problem framing and idea generation:** Develop a clear prompt, solicit stakeholder observations, and assemble a diverse set of statements about barriers, facilitators, and processes of translation.
- **Idea synthesis:** Review, refine, and consolidate statements while preserving important distinctions across perspectives.
- **Sorting and rating:** Ask participants to organize statements by conceptual similarity and assess their importance and other relevant dimensions.
- **Quantitative concept mapping:** Apply multidimensional scaling and hierarchical cluster analysis to produce point maps, cluster maps, and other interpretable representations.
- **Interpretation and comparison:** Engage stakeholders in reviewing the maps and examine where academic, practitioner, and operational perspectives converge or diverge.

These steps are intended to yield an empirically grounded conceptual framework rather than a predetermined taxonomy. The work is designed to integrate qualitative interpretation with statistically structured evidence.

## Collaborative Research Design

The project engages complementary stakeholder groups over the course of the research. An **Advisory Group** helps refine how the problem is framed and which questions deserve attention. A **Core Group** supports development and testing of the framing materials and activities, while a broader **Extended Group** contributes perspectives for the mapping process.

A central design consideration is that questions do more than collect answers: how the problem is framed influences which dimensions stakeholders recognize, which expertise is brought to bear, and which assumptions are challenged. For this reason, the research attends to the design of prompts, participant recruitment, feedback, concept elicitation, and interpretation as important methodological decisions in their own right.

## My Contributions

My contributions focus on research design and coordination, stakeholder engagement, development and evaluation of study materials, and implementation of the Group Concept Mapping workflow. This includes supporting the formulation and testing of project prompts, coordinating feedback from collaborating groups, developing online elicitation and assessment procedures, and preparing the conditions for rigorous analysis of the resulting conceptual structures.

This work extends my broader research on macrocognition, collaborative problem solving, human–AI teaming, and the measurement of processes through which teams generate and integrate knowledge.
